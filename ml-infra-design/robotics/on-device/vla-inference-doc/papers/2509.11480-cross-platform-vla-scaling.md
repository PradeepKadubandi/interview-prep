# Cross-Platform Scaling of Vision-Language-Action Models from Edge to Cloud GPUs

**arXiv:** 2509.11480v2  
**Authors:** Taherin, Lin, A. Akbari, Ar. Akbari, Zhao, Chen, Kaeli, Wang (Northeastern University + EmbodyX)  
**Year:** 2025 (v2: Jan 2026)

---

## Setup

**Models (5):** OpenVLA (7B), SpatialVLA (4B), OpenVLA-OFT (7B), QwenVLA (2.6B, Qwen 2.5-1.5B backbone), and VOTE in three configs (VOTE-1T, VOTE-2T, VOTE-MLP4, all 7B LLaMA 2). VOTE and QwenVLA are the authors' own models. All differ primarily in action head design, output chunk size (1–16), and LLM backbone size.

**Hardware:** Edge — Jetson AGX Orin (204.8 GB/s LPDDR5, up to 60W) at four power modes: MAX, 50W, 30W, 15W. Datacenter — H100 (3.35 TB/s HBM3), A100 (1.6 TB/s HBM2e), A6000 (768 GB/s GDDR6), V100 (900 GB/s HBM2).

**Benchmark:** LIBERO (4 task suites: Spatial, Object, Goal, Long; 200 trials per suite).

**Throughput definition:** actions generated per second (Hz) = chunk\_size / latency\_per\_chunk.

---

## Key Findings

- **Architecture choice dominates hardware tier for throughput.** VOTE-MLP4 reaches 474.78 Hz on H100 — over 64× faster than OpenVLA on the same hardware. The action tokenization strategy and chunk size matter more than upgrading the GPU.

- **Memory footprint is driven almost entirely by backbone and vision encoder size; action head design is negligible.** QwenVLA (2.6B total) uses 7.39 GB VRAM; OpenVLA-OFT (7B) uses 19.20 GB. All VOTE configs (different chunk sizes, MLP depths) land at 14.40–14.45 GB — essentially identical.

- **Throughput degradation with power reduction on Orin is non-linear.** The drop from 50W → 30W is sharper than MAX → 50W, especially for larger/compute-heavy models. QwenVLA retains a higher fraction of its MAX-mode throughput under power reduction compared to 7B models, suggesting smaller backbones are more resilient to power throttling.

- **Modern high-end edge can beat older datacenter hardware.** Orin MAX with VOTE-MLP4 achieves 55.57 Hz, surpassing the V100 (32.28 Hz). The edge vs. datacenter gap is not monotone across GPU generations.

- **Smaller backbone can be accuracy-competitive.** QwenVLA (2.6B) scores 78.8% average on LIBERO, beating full OpenVLA (7B) at 76.5%. VOTE-1T (7B) leads at 96.9%, only slightly ahead of OpenVLA-OFT (95.3%). High accuracy and high throughput are separable: VOTE-MLP4 trades ~3% accuracy vs VOTE-1T for 2× throughput.

- **Edge-to-H100 throughput gap is 4.8–8.5× depending on model size.** VOTE-MLP4 is 8.5× faster on H100 vs Orin MAX; QwenVLA is 4.8×. The gap widens for larger backbones, which benefit more from H100's higher memory bandwidth and parallelism.

## Critique

**[Key gap] No task success rate comparison across hardware tiers.** The most deployment-relevant question this paper could have answered — does a model's task success rate degrade when moving from datacenter to edge hardware? — is entirely absent. If Orin adds ~10× latency per chunk, does slower re-planning frequency or timing mismatch with the physical environment hurt task success? The paper profiles latency and throughput across hardware tiers thoroughly, but accuracy (Table V) is reported only once, with no hardware context. To be fair, a systematic accuracy-vs-hardware study would require real-robot or high-fidelity sim evaluation at each power mode, which is a significantly larger undertaking and may not have been the authors' goal. Nevertheless, it remains the key missing piece for anyone trying to make an actual edge deployment decision.

**Latency unit inconsistency (graphs vs. text).** Figure 2 y-axes are correctly labeled "Latency (s)", with H100 values ranging from 0.03 s to 0.34 s and Orin MAX from 0.29 s to 1.95 s. However, the text in Section IV describes these exact same numbers as milliseconds ("ranging from 0.03 ms … to 0.34 ms" for H100, "0.29 ms … to 1.95 ms" for Orin). The graphs are correct; the prose has a unit error throughout the latency discussion.
